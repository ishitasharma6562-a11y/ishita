Brand Strategy Visual Identity Art Direction

Nazar — making attention feel intentional, not passive.

A brand identity built around a cultural idea everyone already knows — the fear of "nazar lagna" — flipped into something you'd actually want more of.

The Idea

Lagne do.

Nazar was the name that made an immediate, cultural connection to attention. We grow up hearing "nazar na lag jaye" — the fear of too many eyes, too much attention, landing on you at once.

Nazar takes that same idea and flips it: instead of warding off attention, you're inviting it. What used to be something to protect yourself from is now something you pay for.

The Tagline

Seen is easy. Heard is the upgrade.

Nazar already means gaze. But a gaze alone can be empty, passive, meaningless.

Out of the tagline options, "Be heard, not just seen" was the only one that pushed past just being noticed and pointed to something deeper — being registered, valued, responded to.

On Nazar, attention isn't a glance in passing — it's undivided, paid for, and real. That's the difference between being seen and being heard.

Visual Identity

An identity built to hold both meanings at once.

The evil-eye motif carries the "nazar" reference directly, while a deep maroon-and-cream palette and a bold, direct type system keep the brand feeling confident rather than superstitious — attention as something intentional, not accidental.

Colour & motif

Deep maroon grounds the brand in intensity; warm cream keeps it legible and human. The evil-eye shape recurs as a mark of focus.

Voice

Short, declarative lines ("Lagne do.") that sound like they're daring you to look — matching the brand's core flip on attention.
