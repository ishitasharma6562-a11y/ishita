FURLENCO

Designing for Confidence. A Furlenco UX case study

Does renting new furniture feel like adding images in a google document?

When I moved into my first apartment, I needed a study table fast. Renting felt like the obvious choice, and Furlenco was the first platform I turned to. Reviews were good, the brand felt safe.

But three days before delivery, I still didn't know if I'd made the right call.

Furniture isn't like most things you buy online. It's expensive, difficult to return, and expected to last for years, so people aren't just comparing products, they're trying to figure out if something will genuinely fit their space, their lifestyle, their daily routine.

A wrong guess here doesn't just cost money. It costs the hassle of living with it, or the hassle of undoing it.

Was It Just Me?

My own experience gave me a reason to start, but it wasn't proof of anything beyond myself. So I looked at the problem three ways: through my own hesitation, through a 31-person survey and 5 in-depth interviews with people who'd rented or bought furniture online, and through a direct audit of Furlenco's own product.

That became my hypothesis:
Maybe the problem isn't finding furniture. It's feeling certain you've found the right furniture.

How I Approached This

  1. 01

    Personal framing

    Mapped my own rental experience step by step, pinpointing the exact moments doubt crept in, not just "I felt unsure," but where and why.

  2. 02

    Survey & Interviews

    Ran a 31-person survey and 5 in-depth interviews with people who'd rented or bought furniture online, to see where their confidence dipped and why.

  3. 03

    UX audit

    Walked Furlenco's landing, listing, and product pages against six confidence-related questions, checking where the product itself answered or ignored those doubts.

The Confidence Gap

82% of people who rated their confidence a neutral 3/5 went on to hesitate. Only 11% did at 4/5.

Trust Isn't Built by Reviews

Every person distrusted reviews for a different reason. Ignored, too thin, curated, fake.

Photos Are the Real Trust Signal

Real photos build confidence fast; AI-looking or overly polished ones break it just as fast.

10 — Reflection

This project started with one very personal question: "How do I know this furniture is actually right for me?" But the real lesson was that my own experience could only start the investigation — it couldn't be the evidence for the solution.

Research helped me question my assumptions; the audit helped me see the product more objectively; and the redesign taught me to trace every feature back to a real, specific moment of doubt.

Next steps

This redesign is grounded in real research — a 31-person survey and 5 interviews — but it hasn't been tested against actual behavior yet. The next step would be a moderated usability test comparing the current product page against the redesign, tracking:

  • Time to decision — browsing to confirming
  • Confidence shift — out of the 3/5 "hesitation zone"
  • Return-terms lookup — checked without prompting
